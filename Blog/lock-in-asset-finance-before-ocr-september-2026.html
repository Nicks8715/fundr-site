If you're planning to lock in asset finance before September's OCR decision, you're not early — you're on time. The Reserve Bank raised the Official Cash Rate to 2.50% on 8 July 2026, its first hike after nearly a year on hold, and the next Monetary Policy Statement lands on 2 September 2026. Most major bank economists expect further increases toward an estimated neutral rate of around 3.00%.

For NZ business owners planning a vehicle or equipment purchase in the next few months, that timeline matters. Here's what's actually changed, how it flows through to your finance rate, and who should be moving now versus who can reasonably wait.

Where the OCR sits right now

After holding at 2.25% through the back half of 2025 and the first half of 2026, the RBNZ lifted the OCR to 2.50% at its 8 July review. The Reserve Bank's own commentary pointed to ongoing effects from the Middle East conflict keeping inflation elevated in the near term, even as falling fuel prices offered some relief.

The next scheduled review is a full Monetary Policy Statement on 2 September 2026 — one of the more significant dates on the calendar, since MPS decisions come with updated RBNZ forecasts and typically carry more weight than an interim Monetary Policy Review. Bank economists are broadly aligned in expecting the tightening cycle to continue, with the OCR trending toward the RBNZ's estimated neutral setting of roughly 3.00%.

This is a genuine reversal, not noise. For most of 2024 and 2025, the dominant narrative was falling rates. Some competitor content and older articles still circulating reflect that old picture. The current cycle is moving the other way — worth checking the publish date on anything telling you rates are still falling.

Why the September decision matters for your finance

A single 25-basis-point move doesn't sound like much on a business loan or equipment lease. But two things make the September decision worth planning around rather than ignoring:

  • It's not a one-off. July's hike is widely read as the start of a cycle, not an isolated adjustment. If the RBNZ moves again in September, that's a second increase in under two months — a clear directional signal for anyone financing on a fixed rate.
  • Finance rates move ahead of the announcement. Wholesale swap rates — which drive what lenders actually charge on fixed asset and business finance — respond to expectations, not just confirmed decisions. If the market is pricing in a September hike, some of that increase can already be showing up in the rates lenders quote today, before the RBNZ has said a word.

What this looks like on a real deal

Say you're financing a $60,000 piece of equipment over 5 years. A 0.5–0.75 percentage point difference in your rate — entirely plausible across one or two OCR cycles — works out to roughly $1,500–$2,300 in extra interest over the term, depending on structure. Not catastrophic, but not nothing either, and it's the kind of difference that's genuinely avoidable with timing.

The exact number depends on your asset, term, and lender — this is illustrative, not a quote. A broker can run the real numbers against your specific deal.

How asset finance is actually priced

This is the part most business owners don't realise: your equipment or vehicle finance quote isn't tied directly to the OCR. It's priced primarily off wholesale swap rates, which reflect where the market expects interest rates to average over your loan term — not where the OCR sits on the day you apply.

That's why swap rates on terms of 12 months and beyond had already started firming through mid-2026, ahead of the RBNZ's July move. It's also why "the OCR hasn't changed yet" isn't the same as "rates haven't changed yet" — by the time an OCR decision is confirmed, much of the expected move may already be reflected in what lenders are quoting.

We've written a full breakdown of how OCR and swap rates relate if you want the mechanics in more detail — see OCR vs Swap Rates: Why Your Equipment Finance Quote Doesn't Move With the Official Cash Rate.

Who should seriously consider locking in now

  • You have a specific vehicle or equipment purchase planned in the next 1–3 months. If the timing is already close, there's little upside to waiting through further OCR reviews on the chance rates fall — the current trend points the other way.
  • You're financing on a fixed rate over a longer term (4–7 years). Longer terms are more sensitive to swap rate movements, since the lender is pricing in expected rates across the whole period, not just today's setting.
  • You're refinancing existing finance taken out during the 2023–2024 high-rate period. This is a different calculation — you may still be better off than your original rate even after recent increases — but it's worth getting a proper comparison rather than assuming either way.
  • Cash flow certainty matters more than chasing the lowest possible number. If a rate rise would genuinely strain your repayments, locking in known costs now is often the more sensible call than gambling on a pause.

Who can reasonably afford to wait

  • Your purchase is genuinely 6+ months out. A lot can shift between now and then, and locking in today doesn't help if the asset isn't ready to buy.
  • You're financing on a variable rate or short-term facility. Variable structures move with the market either way, so timing the fixed-rate decision matters less.
  • You'd rather see the September MPS confirmed before committing. There's nothing wrong with wanting more certainty first — just be aware that waiting is itself a rate bet, not a neutral choice.

Common questions about the September OCR decision and asset finance

What is the OCR sitting at right now?
The Reserve Bank of New Zealand raised the OCR to 2.50% on 8 July 2026. The next scheduled review is a Monetary Policy Statement on 2 September 2026. Most bank economists expect the RBNZ to continue moving the OCR toward its estimated neutral rate of around 3.00% over the coming reviews.
Does asset finance move directly with the OCR?
Not directly. Asset and business finance rates are primarily priced off wholesale swap rates, which reflect where the market expects the OCR to average over the loan term — not where it sits today. Swap rates typically move in anticipation of RBNZ decisions, which is why fixed asset finance rates can rise before an official OCR announcement.
Should I lock in my asset finance rate now?
If you're planning a vehicle or equipment purchase in the next few months and financing on a fixed rate, locking in before further OCR increases are priced in can protect you from a higher rate later. It depends on your specific timeline, asset, and cash flow — a broker can review your situation and tell you honestly whether waiting or locking in makes more sense.
What happens to my rate if I wait until after September?
If the RBNZ raises the OCR again on 2 September, and swap rates have already adjusted in anticipation, fixed asset finance rates quoted after that date may be higher than rates available today — particularly on longer terms. There's no guarantee rates will be higher, but the current trend and market commentary both point that direction.
Can I lock in a rate before I've chosen the exact asset?
Some lenders allow pre-approval with a rate held for a defined period while you finalise the vehicle or equipment purchase. This varies by lender and isn't available in every case. A broker can tell you which lenders on the panel offer this and for how long.
