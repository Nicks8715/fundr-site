The Reserve Bank raised the Official Cash Rate by 25 basis points to 2.75% on 2 September 2026. It's the second increase since the RBNZ resumed tightening in July, when it lifted the OCR to 2.50%, and the Bank has said it may need to raise it further this year. If you're weighing up whether to lock in asset finance now, the decision has moved from "before the hike" to "before the next one".

For NZ business owners planning a vehicle or equipment purchase in the next few months, here's what's changed, how it flows through to your finance rate, and who should be moving now versus who can reasonably wait.

Where the OCR sits now

The OCR was 2.50% after the RBNZ's July increase and moved to 2.75% at the 2 September Monetary Policy Statement. The Bank's reasoning was about inflation: it rose to 4.1% in the June 2026 quarter, driven largely by higher fuel prices linked to the Middle East conflict, and the RBNZ expects it to stay above 3% for the rest of 2026 before easing back toward its 1–3% target range next year.

On direction, the Bank said it may need to increase the OCR further this year. RNZ reported that the statement's interest rate guidance pointed to at least one more 25-basis-point rise, to 3%, by the end of the year, and possibly two more by the middle of next year. That's guidance, not a commitment. The RBNZ hasn't locked in a path, and the next OCR update is on 28 October 2026.

Check the date on anything you read about rates. Through 2024 and 2025 the dominant story was falling rates, and plenty of older articles still reflect that. The direction since July 2026 is up.

What the 2.75% OCR means for your finance

A single 25-basis-point move doesn't sound like much on a business loan or equipment lease. Two things make it worth planning around:

  • It's a run of increases, not a one-off. Two hikes in two months, with the Bank signalling more may be needed, is a clear directional signal for anyone financing on a fixed rate.
  • Finance rates move ahead of the announcement. Wholesale swap rates, which drive what lenders charge on fixed asset and business finance, respond to expectations rather than only confirmed decisions. Part of any further expected increase can already be reflected in the rates lenders quote today.

What this looks like on a real deal

Say you're financing a $60,000 piece of equipment over 5 years. A difference of 0.5–0.75 percentage points in your rate, which is plausible across one or two more OCR moves, works out to roughly $900–$1,300 in extra interest over the term, depending on structure. Not catastrophic, but it's the kind of difference that's avoidable with timing.

The exact number depends on your asset, term and lender. This is illustrative, not a quote. A broker can run the real numbers against your specific deal.

How asset finance is actually priced

This is the part most business owners don't realise: your equipment or vehicle finance quote isn't tied directly to the OCR. It's priced primarily off wholesale swap rates, which reflect where the market expects interest rates to average over your loan term, not where the OCR sits on the day you apply.

That's why "the OCR hasn't changed yet" isn't the same as "rates haven't changed yet", and why a fixed rate doesn't necessarily jump the day the RBNZ announces a move. By the time a decision is confirmed, much of the expected change may already be in the quote.

We've written a full breakdown of how the two relate if you want the mechanics in more detail: OCR vs Swap Rates: Why Your Equipment Finance Quote Doesn't Move With the Official Cash Rate.

Who should seriously consider locking in now

  • You have a specific vehicle or equipment purchase planned in the next 1–3 months. If the timing is already close, there's little upside in waiting through further OCR updates on the chance rates fall. The current guidance points the other way.
  • You're financing on a fixed rate over a longer term (4–7 years). Longer terms are more sensitive to swap rate movements, since the lender is pricing in expected rates across the whole period.
  • You're refinancing existing finance taken out during the 2023–2024 high-rate period. This is a different calculation. You may still be better off than your original rate even after recent increases, but it's worth getting a proper comparison rather than assuming either way.
  • Cash flow certainty matters more than chasing the lowest possible number. If a rate rise would genuinely strain your repayments, locking in known costs now is often the more sensible call than hoping for a pause.

Who can reasonably afford to wait

  • Your purchase is genuinely 6+ months out. A lot can shift between now and then, and locking in today doesn't help if the asset isn't ready to buy.
  • You're financing on a variable rate or short-term facility. Variable structures move with the market either way, so timing a fixed-rate decision matters less.
  • You'd rather see the 28 October update before committing. There's nothing wrong with wanting more certainty first. Just be aware that waiting is itself a rate bet, not a neutral choice.

There's no universally "right" answer here. It depends on your specific timeline and asset. fundr will give you a straight read on your situation, not a generic push to sign today.

Common questions about the 2.75% OCR and asset finance

What is the OCR sitting at right now?
The Reserve Bank of New Zealand raised the OCR by 25 basis points to 2.75% on 2 September 2026. It was the second increase since the RBNZ resumed tightening in July, when it lifted the OCR to 2.50%. The next OCR update is on 28 October 2026.
Does asset finance move directly with the OCR?
Not directly. Asset and business finance rates are primarily priced off wholesale swap rates, which reflect where the market expects the OCR to average over the loan term, not where it sits today. Swap rates typically move in anticipation of RBNZ decisions, which is why fixed asset finance rates can change before an official OCR announcement.
Should I lock in my asset finance rate now?
If you're planning a vehicle or equipment purchase in the next few months and financing on a fixed rate, locking in now protects you if the RBNZ raises the OCR again. It depends on your specific timeline, asset and cash flow. A broker can review your situation and tell you honestly whether waiting or locking in makes more sense.
What happens to my rate if I wait until the next OCR review?
The RBNZ has said it may need to increase the OCR further this year, but it has not committed to a path. If it does raise the OCR again and swap rates have already adjusted, fixed asset finance rates quoted afterwards may be higher than today's, particularly on longer terms. There is no guarantee either way.
Can I lock in a rate before I've chosen the exact asset?
Some lenders allow pre-approval with a rate held for a defined period while you finalise the vehicle or equipment purchase. This varies by lender and isn't available in every case. A broker can tell you which lenders on the panel offer this and for how long.
